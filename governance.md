**v0.0.2 · 2026-10-14 · Request for Comments**

Swartzism is a doctrine, not a membership. It has no leaders, no signatories, and no one to obey. It has a text, a record, and a process for changing both. This document is that process.

## 1. What publication means

The doctrine ships as a Request for Comments. Version 0.0.1 is an opening position, not a finished one: every line is contestable by argument, and the version number records what happened to the argument.

## 2. What is fixed

One thing. **The boundary — liberate the world's knowledge, defend each person's life — is constitutional.** It is not subject to ordinary amendment, coalition bargaining, or cost-benefit override. Proposals to move it are out of scope by definition; the doctrine's answer to them is already written. Everything else is revisable.

## 3. Versioning

- **0.0.x** — textual: wording, clarity, source corrections, corrigenda.
- **0.x.0** — structural: changes to the works, the guardrails, the taxonomy, or the framing of an axiom.
- **1.0.0** — only when the doctrine has survived a full adversarial pass (Pass 2 minimum) with no open amendment proposals against its axioms. Not before.
- Versions are tags, not authorities. No version is binding on anyone; the text argues for itself.

## 4. How to participate

The repository is https://github.com/LogicIncZo/swartzism. Three doors, in rising order of commitment:

- **Comment** — open an issue against the repository. Name the line you are attacking, state the argument, and cite the evidence.
- **Amend** — open a pull request carrying the exact changed text. Proposals move at the speed of §5.
- **Fork** — the preferred act. The license invites it: take the texts whole, change what you can defend, run your own review, and republish under CC BY-SA with the boundary intact. A fork is not a schism; it is the doctrine replicating the way information replicates — by copying and variation. If your fork survives its own attacks, link it back: the version history is the commons' memory.

Two rules bind every door:

- Claims about the record carry source URLs or they do not count. This rule binds the doctrine's own texts first — the review log records the corrigenda, including one fabricated citation caught and removed before publication.
- Arguments are judged, not authors. The doctrine is apersonal: contributions carry no attribution inside the texts, and no one's endorsement is evidence.

## 5. What moves a proposal

1. The proposal names the exact text it changes.
2. The changed text passes the profession test (Principles §5.4): could someone safely say it aloud at work?
3. The change survives an adversarial pass — an attempt to break it from the record, from the boundary, and from practice.
4. If all three hold, the version advances and the rationale is logged in the review log.

## 6. The self-review rule

The doctrine is required to attack itself. The adversarial review log is not optional apparatus; it is the doctrine eating its own cooking. A version of the text that has not survived a published attempt to destroy it is not stable, whatever its number.

## 7. What governance cannot do

Governance can change wording, structure, and claims about the record. It cannot: (a) move the boundary; (b) name leaders; (c) convert the doctrine into a membership, an organization, or a brand. If the doctrine ever needs permission slips, it has stopped being a doctrine.
