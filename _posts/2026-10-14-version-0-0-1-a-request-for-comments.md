Today the doctrine ships — not as a finished thing, but as an opening position. Version 0.0.1 of Swartzism is published as a Request for Comments: five axioms, an ethics of scale, one boundary, five works, and a process for changing all of it except the last.

Read [the Manifesto](/swartzism/manifesto/). Then read [the Principles](/swartzism/principles/) — the analytical machine underneath: every claim about the record carries a source, and one fabricated citation has already been caught, corrected, and logged. Then read [the Governance](/swartzism/governance/) — how the text changes, what never changes, and why.

Three commitments come with the version number:

1. **The boundary is constitutional.** Liberate the world's knowledge; defend each person's life. Everything else in the text is revisable by argument. The boundary is not revisable by coalition, cost-benefit, or mood.
2. **The text must survive its own attacks.** A version that has not survived a published attempt to destroy it is not stable, whatever its number. The review log is the doctrine eating its own cooking — and Pass 2 is now public business.
3. **No one owns it.** The doctrine is apersonal: arguments are judged, not authors. It publishes under its own name, takes help, not direction, and carries no signatures.

The profession test governs its texts: a doctrine that taxes its professors shrinks to a church of martyrs. This one intends to grow — which is why 0.0.1 is an invitation, not a proclamation.

Attack it where it is weakest. [That is what the comment process is for.](/swartzism/governance/)
