The word does not exist in the record. Aaron Swartz never named a movement, never wrote a manifesto that founded an "-ism", never claimed disciples. "Swartzism" is a construction — a name for the question: **what does the record imply?**

So the honest opening move is to define the term the way this blog will use it, and then say what it is *not*.

## The raw material

Swartz left behind an unusually legible body of work: essays on [Raw Thought](http://www.aaronsw.com/weblog/) written between 2002 and his death in 2013, the [Guerrilla Open Access Manifesto](https://archive.org/details/GuerillaOpenAccessManifesto) (2008), the open-access advocacy, the [PACER protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Swartz#PACER_case) with Carl Malamud, the JSTOR mass download of 4.8 million articles that led to his prosecution and suicide, and the [Semmelweis essay](https://github.com/joshleitzel/rawthought) on how hard it is to see what is in front of you. (The [Bad Apple](https://www.aaronswartzday.org/bad-apple-announcement/) police-accountability tool suite, launched in 2021 by the Aaron Swartz Day Police Surveillance Project, carries his name and ethos — a separate project, built in his memory.)

A doctrine built from this record has to be built *from* it: every claim traceable to a source, every failure admitted.

## Three axioms, one boundary

The doctrine this blog will argue for rests on three claims and one boundary.

**1. Knowledge about the world is a commons.** Facts, laws, research, records — these are produced collectively and their enclosure is a harm. The [manifesto](https://archive.org/details/GuerillaOpenAccessManifesto) was about scientific literature, but the logic extends: when a society's own records (court filings, gazettes, registries) are priced and gated, the society is taxing itself on its own memory.

**2. Information is power, and power must be accountable.** The PACER action treated the *free flow* of judicial records as a form of accountability. The Bad Apple project treated the *documentation* of police misconduct as accountability. Information-as-power cuts both ways: it is why institutions hoard it, and why publics should pry it loose.

**3. Privacy is a power too — held by the powerless.** This is the boundary, and it is load-bearing. Data *about persons* is not a commons. The person described has a claim that the institution describing them does not. When a leak contains personal data, the doctrine's instruction is not "liberate it" — it is *protect it, and investigate the system that leaked it.*

That last point is the one people get wrong about Swartzism, so it deserves its own post. [Read it next](/swartzism/2026/08/the-pii-question/).

## What Swartzism is not

- **Not hagiography.** Swartz was [arrested in 2011 after a physical confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Swartz#Arrest_and_prosecution) over a hidden camera at an MIT network closet — an escalation that did not serve his cause. A doctrine that requires its subject to be flawless is an idol, not an argument. The record includes the failures; the argument has to survive them.
- **Not "steal everything."** The boundary above is the entire point. Swartzism without the PII boundary is just piracy with a sad story attached.
- **Not a cult.** No membership, no orthodoxy, no leader. An argument that can be stress-tested is the only kind worth having.

The name is a convenience. The argument is the thing.
